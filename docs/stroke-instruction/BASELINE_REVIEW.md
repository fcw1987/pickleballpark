## Findings

- **Forehand — fails clear instructional silhouette.** At late preparation/contact, the dominant elbow juts sideways and back while the face hangs below the hand near the hip. The shoulder line stays mostly frontal instead of visibly loading on the paddle side. Follow-through then rises across the chest. Ball/face meeting exists, but the body's preparation and arm path are not clear enough to teach the stroke.
- **Backhand — fails stroke identity.** Its paddle begins across the body, but at exact contact it returns to the dominant-hand side, resembling forehand contact. The finish is cramped near the shoulder. Correct contact side and approach/support position first; greater amplitude alone would preserve the wrong signature.
- **Lob — fails low-to-high preparation.** The paddle begins around waist/chest level and falls into a low contact before rising. The body's load/extension is almost absent. Give it a lower preparation and a sustained rising contact-to-finish path with controlled face orientation.
- **Support base — visually weak.** The same largely square foot arrangement and crouch persist through all three sequences, with little visible transfer. The footwear appears detached from the court in some frames, but this is a perception observation: sneaker geometry begins at local Y=0, and the nearby ball shadow can be confused with a foot shadow. Verify world sole height, court height, and shadow attachment before changing geometry.
- **No-label comparison — fails ready recognition.** The rows differ somewhat in paddle finish, but contact silhouettes are too similar, particularly forehand versus backhand. Lob's high destination alone does not make its preparation distinct. Left-handed rows show the same broad problem rather than solving it.

## Acceptance status

Baseline not accepted for instructional stroke quality. Preserve the accepted environment and character design. Candidate must be reviewed using the same fixtures/cameras, with final approach and segment boundaries added where possible. Actual normal/slow playback or dense ordered frame evidence remains necessary for smoothness and foot-lock acceptance; isolated contact screenshots and unit tests cannot establish that.
