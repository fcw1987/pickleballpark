# Pickleball Park — fidelity review wave 1

Review date: 2026-09-14. The journey used fresh Playwright browser contexts with `serviceWorkers: 'block'` against `http://127.0.0.1:4184/`. Viewports were simulated at 390×844, 320×568, 844×390, and 1440×900, at 1× and selected 2× device scale. A physical phone, tablet, or high-DPI display was unavailable; these are browser simulations only.

The first capture pass was completed before the running app moved to `4fc3195`; those files use the original viewport names such as [desktop1440x900-initial.png](review-wave1/desktop1440x900-initial.png). The current pass after that iteration uses the `current-*` files, for example [current-desktop1440-initial.png](review-wave1/current-desktop1440-initial.png). The menu/edit evidence was collected on the current build.

## Journey tried

I began as a recreational doubles player with no prior instruction. The landing state is a pre-authored play titled “Starter Serve, Return & Third,” at “Step 1. Starting Positions,” with a 3D court, four players, a yellow ball, and a magenta target ring. I pressed Play and watched the timeline progress from Serve; after roughly 1.2 seconds the control read Pause and the timeline was at about 1.4–1.5 seconds. I switched to 2D while the rally was running. The view landed on the later “3. Drop” shot, where the yellow ball, yellow path, and player positions made the current shot legible. I switched back to 3D at the end of the 3.7-second authored sequence. Evidence: [desktop-play-1200ms.png](review-wave1/desktop-play-1200ms.png), [desktop-2d.png](review-wave1/desktop-2d.png), and [desktop-3d-again.png](review-wave1/desktop-3d-again.png).

I opened the visible Menu. It exposed the authored-play structure and the routes for Rename play, New Play, My Plays, Save As, Learn, Court Planner, Import / Export, Help, and Appearance. The menu is coherent with the pixel treatment, but it reads like a desktop product menu rather than a game pause menu. Evidence: [desktop-menu-open2.png](review-wave1/desktop-menu-open2.png) and [phone320-menu-open2.png](review-wave1/phone320-menu-open2.png).

I opened Edit shot on a fresh 390×844 context. The compact details panel initially showed Shot family, Hitter, target guidance, quick target buttons, and a collapsed “Details · coordinates, contact, movement” group. I opened that group, changed Movement intent from Hold to Advance, and selected Place movement on court. The UI entered a clear pin mode with “Tap a movement destination to pin this player,” a Cancel movement button, and a visible “Green 1 pin.” Evidence: [phone-details-expanded.png](review-wave1/phone-details-expanded.png), [phone-movement-advance3.png](review-wave1/phone-movement-advance3.png), and [phone-place-movement3.png](review-wave1/phone-place-movement3.png). ## Findings

The visual identity is strong. The court, players, signage, palette, shadows, and controls form a consistent pixel world with a clear authored point of view. At desktop size it looks like a polished pixel-game editor or coaching toy. It does not yet feel like a finished player-facing game because the builder shell is always present, the first-run task is implied rather than explained in the play area, and the responsive control strip becomes visibly unstable at landscape phone size.

The clearest web-form cues are Edit, + Add, long select-style shot settings, numeric target fields, Save As, Import / Export, and the menu labels. That is appropriate for a play builder, but the transition between “watch a rally” and “author a rally” is not announced. A recreational player can start Play, but they must infer that the product is a builder from the surrounding controls.

Court attention is generally well prioritized at 1440×900: the large court and player sprites dominate, while the target ring and yellow ball are strong focal markers. On the 390×844 and 320×568 views, the court still fills the available middle region and the ball remains findable, although it is small near a player’s hand at the starting state. The target ring has stronger contrast than the ball. In 2D during the Drop shot, the ball and yellow flight line are easy to follow. Outside-court grass is a large low-information field, but its texture keeps it from reading as an accidental blank panel.

The current build’s most serious fidelity issue is the 844×390 landscape header. [current-landscape844-initial.png](review-wave1/current-landscape844-initial.png) shows “Starter draft · not saved yet,” “SHOT 1,” the Serve/high-arc description, the chapter control, Edit, Add, and view controls competing in one strip; “SHOT 1” and the shot description visibly collide. The same strip is much more legible at 1440×900 in [current-desktop1440-initial.png](review-wave1/current-desktop1440-initial.png), and the narrow phone treatment is more disciplined in [current-phone390-initial.png](review-wave1/current-phone390-initial.png) and [current-phone320-initial.png](review-wave1/current-phone320-initial.png), although 320 px reduces Menu and the chapter control to icon/short-label treatments.

The least intentional moments are the compressed top-bar labels and low-emphasis action affordances at narrow sizes. At 320 px, the first bar exposes only a hamburger, “SHOT 1,” Edit, a plus glyph, and 3D/2D; a new player has to infer that the plus is Add. At landscape size, controls have enough room in aggregate but no layout rule prevents them from overlapping. The pixel font remains readable in the court and bottom playback bar, but small status and descriptive copy become difficult to scan when several chips sit adjacent.

Advanced controls are findable after a small amount of exploration. The visible Details disclosure leads to flight, receiver, contact style, serve method, precision, and movement controls; Place movement on court is a meaningful direct-manipulation route. The interaction itself is understandable once entered because the pin instruction and Cancel movement state are explicit. Playback options are tucked behind a vertical-ellipsis disclosure in the bottom bar, which is discoverable for experienced users but not self-explanatory for a first-time recreational player.

The menu is visually cohesive and its grouping is now clearer: play identity, practice, tools, and appearance are distinguishable in [desktop-menu-open2.png](review-wave1/desktop-menu-open2.png). Saving remains conceptually indirect. The menu exposes Save As and the header reports “Starter draft · not saved yet”; after edit/add interactions the state reports “Saved on this device,” but there is no prominent Save action beside Done or the movement controls. A user can discover that the app persists locally, yet may hesitate over whether Done, Add, or placing a pin commits the change.

## Strongest fixes

1. Give the landscape header a responsive layout contract. At 844×390, wrap or collapse the status, shot description, chapter, Edit/Add, and 2D/3D controls so text never overlaps. Preserve a readable chapter label and one clearly labeled Add action at the narrowest width.

2. Make the watch-versus-build state explicit on entry. Keep the pixel court as the hero, but add a short first-run cue near the playback bar such as “Play the authored serve, then edit a shot,” so a recreational player understands the product’s purpose without opening Help.

3. Surface the current shot’s semantic summary during playback. The 2D Drop state is understandable because it shows a path; the starting Serve state relies on the title and target marker. A compact, persistent “Serve · high arc · deep left” summary would reduce the need to infer the shot from sprites and controls.

4. Make persistence legible at the point of action. Keep the current “Saved on this device” status, but pair Done and movement placement with a short saved/unsaved confirmation and expose Save/Save As semantics in one predictable place. This is especially important after a user changes movement intent or adds a shot.

5. Keep narrow controls labeled where they carry intent. The 320 px plus glyph and shortened shot label save space but make Add and chapter navigation less discoverable. Use an overflow grouping or a compact labeled control with a tooltip/accessible name that is also visible on touch layouts.

Harness note: blocking service workers produced the expected registration failure in the page console; this was imposed by the requested fresh-context test setup and is not treated as an app finding. No source or core app files were edited, and no external messages, deployment, merge, or commit was performed.
