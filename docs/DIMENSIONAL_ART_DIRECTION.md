# Dimensional sports-park direction

This candidate supersedes the old billboard-only replay direction. The editable 2D board, rally authoring, canonical coordinates, trajectory physics and shared playback clock remain authoritative.

## Visual decision

Actual baseline review at 1440×900 and 390×844 showed that the court already has perspective, but the upright eight-direction athletes read as rigid stickers: dark clothing overwhelms team colors, their silhouettes barely change, and action depends on distorting one paddle layer. The phone replay description panel consumes roughly one quarter of its height. A repaint alone does not satisfy the requested dimensional transformation.

The new replay uses original articulated, faceted human athletes inside a compact, walkable sports plaza. The JRPG influence comes from decisive silhouettes, colored cloth panels, restrained stepped texture details and deliberate spatial composition. No Pokémon or Nintendo assets are used.

## Athletes

- Approximately 1.76 meters and five to five-and-a-half heads tall: compact athletic humans, with tapered jerseys, separate shorts, visible knees/shins and light sneakers.
- Broad green or orange jersey panels dominate the torso. Navy is used for shorts, hair/cap and outlines rather than covering the entire figure. Cream piping and small pixel details establish original uniforms.
- Different cap/hair/headband and skin palettes create individual identity without reducing team readability.
- Feet separate into a wide ready stance; knees flex, torso turns, support arm balances and paddle arm prepares, contacts and follows through continuously.
- World orientation follows the shot and opponent. Contact IK moves the visible paddle to the authoritative trajectory point; it never moves the ball or changes shot timing.
- Native scene resolution stays sharp. No coarse whole-frame pixelation. Avoid smooth capsule toys, oversized bobble heads, rigid cuboids and pasted flat figures.

## World and composition

- Teal acrylic court, sage kitchens, warm ivory lines, charcoal net mesh and light tape, with original restrained pixel material details.
- Court edges and net equipment have real volume. Sparse layered tree canopies, pavers and benches form a walkable park; avoid a thick toy-display plinth or distracting dense scenery.
- Court and active athletes are the hero. Initial framing should retain the baseline's useful full-court bounds, then improve actor and ball legibility without clipping playable areas.
- Portrait favors a clear elevated full court. Compact controls should aim near 108–130 pixels, with longer instructions collapsible or scrollable. Small type and disabled-looking controls are unacceptable.
- Warm ivory UI, desaturated navy keylines, jade primary actions and burnt orange secondary accents. Citron highlights are reserved chiefly for the ball and active timing cues. Use restrained pixel corners, a single keyline and small hard shadow.

## Acceptance by rendered evidence

Compare desktop and phone at the same time and camera as baseline. Inspect the actual court view, not only a character close-up. Also inspect at least prepare/contact/follow-through on serve, groundstroke and kitchen exchange; inspect travel and recovery. Check all four camera identities and both handedness values.

A passing result must show visibly dimensional human silhouettes, meaningful full-body pose changes, grounded feet, attached paddles and an easy-to-track ball. The court should fill the useful viewport, and phone controls must remain legible and operable. Automated geometry or frame-cadence checks support this judgment but cannot replace it.
